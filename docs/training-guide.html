DLSCM Platform — User & Training Guide

Disaster Logistics & Supply Chain Management • Comprehensive training for all platform users

Platform Overview

The DLSCM Platform is a web-based system designed for humanitarian organizations managing disaster response logistics. It covers the full lifecycle from preparedness through response, recovery, and resilience building.

25 Modules

Covering inventory, shipments, forecasting, risk modeling, coordination, and more

5 ML Models

Demand forecasting, risk prediction, anomaly detection, disruption analysis

6 Languages

English, Arabic, French, Spanish, Chinese, Russian with RTL support

Offline-First

Continue working without internet. Data syncs automatically when reconnected

Platform Architecture

Phase 1: Foundation→ Phase 2: Intelligence→ Phase 3: Scale→ Phase 4: Ecosystem

Phase 1 covers core inventory, supply requests, shipments, and the operational dashboard. Phase 2 adds AI-powered forecasting, route optimization, and crisis intelligence. Phase 3 introduces prepositioning, the surge marketplace, and recovery planning. Phase 4 provides simulation, risk modeling, and community resilience tools.

Login & Authentication

The platform uses JWT (JSON Web Token) authentication. There are two ways to log in:

Demo Login (Training & Evaluation)

  1. Navigate to the platform URL. The login page appears.
  2. The Demo Login tab is selected by default. You see a list of 8 demo users.
  3. Click any user card to instantly log in as that user. Each user has a different role and organization.
  4. You are redirected to the Dashboard.
ⓘ
For training, try logging in as different roles to see how the platform looks from each perspective. Start with Amina Osei (Ops Director, IFRC) for the fullest view.

Credential Login (Production)

  1. Click the Sign In tab.
  2. Enter your email address and password.
  3. Click Sign In. If credentials are valid, you are redirected to the Dashboard.
  4. If the login fails, an error message appears below the form. Check your credentials and try again.

Session Management

  • Sessions persist across browser refreshes via stored tokens.
  • If your session expires, the platform automatically attempts a token refresh.
  • If the refresh fails, you are redirected to the login page.
  • Click Log Out in the top-right corner to end your session.

Demand Forecasting

OPS DIRECTOR SUPPLY MGR

AI-powered demand prediction using Gradient Boosting with time-series decomposition. The model forecasts daily supply needs across 6 categories.

Generating a Forecast
  1. Navigate to Demand Forecasting.
  2. Select a disaster from the dropdown.
  3. Choose a forecast horizon: 7 days (short-term), 14 days (medium), or 30 days (planning).
  4. The system runs the ML model and displays results within a few seconds.
  5. View summary cards for each category (food, water, medical, shelter, hygiene, NFI) showing total predicted demand.
  6. The line chart below shows daily demand with trend visualization.
Understanding the Model

The forecasting engine uses several advanced techniques:

  • GradientBoostingRegressor — 80 estimators trained on synthesized scenario data matching the disaster type and severity.
  • STL Decomposition — Separates the forecast into trend, seasonal (7-day cycle), and residual components. Seasonal strength and trend direction are reported.
  • Cross-Model Intelligence — Risk scores from the Risk Dashboard and supplier disruption predictions are automatically fed as input features, so forecasts adjust when risk conditions change.
  • Confidence Intervals — Upper and lower bounds (95%) derived from residual standard deviation, widening as the forecast horizon extends.
ⓘ
The response includes feature_importances showing which inputs most influence the prediction, and cross_model_inputs showing the risk_multiplier and supply_disruption_risk values used.

Prepositioning Optimization

OPS DIRECTOR SUPPLY MGR

AI-optimized recommendations for where to preposition emergency supplies, based on stochastic Monte Carlo simulation of 200 disaster scenarios.

Reading Recommendations
  1. Navigate to Prepositioning in the sidebar.
  2. The system automatically analyzes all warehouses against 8 global risk zones.
  3. The coverage map shows warehouse locations (blue) and risk zones (red/yellow).
  4. Below the map, recommendation cards are ranked by coverage score.
  5. Each card shows: rank, warehouse name, location, coverage score, and zones covered.
  6. Click Expand to see detailed stock suggestions (category, priority, reason), current stock by category, and the risk zone coverage table.
Understanding the Optimization

Each recommendation includes optimization metadata:

  • Method — Stochastic scenario-based optimization
  • Expected Value — Average coverage across 200 simulated scenarios
  • CVaR 5% — Conditional Value at Risk: expected coverage in the worst 5% of scenarios
  • Robustness Score — CVaR / Expected Value × 100. Higher means more consistent performance.
  • Supply Chain Risk — Factor derived from supplier disruption predictions. Values >1.0 indicate elevated supply risk.

Offline Mode

ALL ROLES

The platform is designed for field conditions where internet connectivity may be intermittent.

How Offline Mode Works

  1. When your device loses connectivity, an orange Offline chip appears in the top bar.
  2. You can continue creating supply requests — they are saved to IndexedDB (local browser storage).
  3. Pending changes show a count badge on the offline chip (e.g., "3 pending").
  4. When connectivity returns, the platform automatically syncs all pending changes to the server.
  5. Conflicts (if another user modified the same record) are resolved using CRDT (Conflict-free Replicated Data Types) — the last write wins based on hybrid logical clock timestamps.
⚠
Offline mode currently supports supply requests and CRDT-based data only. Other modules (inventory edits, shipment updates) require connectivity.

Common Workflows

Workflow: Emergency Supply Request (End-to-End)
  1. Field Coordinator creates a new supply request from the field, selecting the disaster, priority (Critical), destination, and needed items.
  2. Ops Director reviews the request on the Dashboard (Pending Requests stat card) and changes status to Approved.
  3. Supply Manager checks inventory across warehouses (Cross-Org Visibility) to identify the nearest stock.
  4. Supply Manager uses Route Optimization to determine the best delivery route and transport mode.
  5. Supply Manager creates a shipment linked to the request, selecting the origin warehouse and carrier.
  6. Supply Manager updates stock quantities in Inventory (deducting shipped amounts).
  7. Field Coordinator tracks the shipment on the Shipments map and updates status to Delivered upon arrival.
  8. Compliance Officer verifies the audit trail and generates a donor report for the response period.
Workflow: Disaster Preparedness Planning
  1. Ops Director reviews the Risk Dashboard to identify high-risk regions.
  2. Ops Director runs simulations for potential disaster scenarios in those regions.
  3. Supply Manager reviews Prepositioning recommendations to determine optimal warehouse stock placement.
  4. Supply Manager reviews the Supplier Registry and checks disruption predictions for critical suppliers.
  5. Ops Director uses Demand Forecasting on historical disasters of similar type/severity to estimate supply needs.
  6. Supply Manager adjusts inventory levels based on forecasts and prepositioning recommendations.
  7. All roles complete the Community Resilience Toolkit checklist items.
Workflow: Post-Disaster Recovery
  1. Ops Director creates a Recovery Plan linked to the disaster, setting a target completion date.
  2. Ops Director advances through the 5 phases: Assessment → Stabilization → Short-Term → Long-Term → Resilience.
  3. Supply Manager adds procurement items to the recovery plan with quantities, costs, and supplier assignments.
  4. Compliance Officer creates an After-Action Review capturing: response time, cost per beneficiary, stockout events, lessons learned.
  5. Compliance Officer generates donor reports for the full response period.
  6. Ops Director reviews lessons and feeds them into simulation scenarios for future preparedness.
Workflow: Surge Capacity Activation
  1. A sudden disaster exceeds your organization's capacity.
  2. Ops Director navigates to Surge Marketplace and filters by needed capacity type (warehouse, transport, or personnel).
  3. Click Find Matches to see which listings best fit your needs.
  4. Click Book on the best match, specifying quantity and notes.
  5. The listing owner is notified and can confirm or decline.
  6. Track all bookings in the My Bookings section. Cancel when no longer needed.

Glossary

3W (Who-What-Where)

A coordination framework tracking which organizations (Who) are conducting which activities (What) in which locations (Where). Prevents duplication and identifies gaps.

CAP (Common Alerting Protocol)

An XML standard for exchanging emergency alerts across different warning systems.

CRDT (Conflict-free Replicated Data Type)

A data structure that allows distributed systems to merge concurrent edits without conflicts. Used in DLSCM's offline sync engine.

CVaR (Conditional Value at Risk)

A risk measure indicating the expected outcome in the worst X% of scenarios. The platform uses CVaR 5% for prepositioning analysis.

EDXL-DE (Emergency Data Exchange Language — Distribution Element)

A standard XML envelope for routing emergency messages between organizations.

EWMA (Exponentially Weighted Moving Average)

A time-series smoothing technique that gives more weight to recent observations. Used for detecting consumption drift.

FEFO (First Expiry, First Out)

Inventory rotation principle ensuring items closest to expiry are used first.

GDACS (Global Disaster Alerting Coordination System)

A UN framework providing real-time disaster alerts and impact estimations.

HLC (Hybrid Logical Clock)

A clock algorithm combining physical timestamps with logical counters to provide globally unique, causally ordered timestamps.

Isolation Forest

An unsupervised ML algorithm for anomaly detection that isolates outliers by randomly partitioning data.

JWT (JSON Web Token)

A compact, URL-safe token format used for authentication. Contains encoded user identity and expiration.

LWW (Last-Writer-Wins)

A conflict resolution strategy where the most recent write (by timestamp) takes precedence.

NFI (Non-Food Items)

Household and personal items distributed in emergencies: blankets, cooking sets, jerry cans, sleeping mats.

OSRM (Open Source Routing Machine)

An open-source routing engine for computing shortest paths on road networks.

Sitrep (Situation Report)

A periodic report summarizing the current state of an emergency, including needs, response activities, and gaps.

SKU (Stock Keeping Unit)

A unique identifier assigned to each distinct item in the supply catalog for tracking and ordering.

Sphere Standards

The humanitarian charter and minimum standards in disaster response, covering water, food, shelter, and health.

STL Decomposition

FAQ

How do I change my password?

Click your name in the top bar and use the change-password option. You need your current password and a new password.

What happens if I lose connectivity while creating a request?

The request is saved locally in your browser (IndexedDB). When connectivity returns, it syncs automatically. The orange offline chip in the top bar shows pending items.

How accurate is the demand forecasting?

The model uses GradientBoosting trained on scenario data calibrated to real humanitarian consumption rates (Sphere Standards). It includes 95% confidence intervals. Use these intervals for planning buffers. The model improves as more response data accumulates.

Can I export reports?

Generated donor reports display in the browser. You can use your browser's print function (Ctrl+P / Cmd+P) to save as PDF.

How does the platform handle data from multiple organizations?

Each user belongs to one organization. Data visibility is managed at the warehouse/stock level. The Cross-Org Visibility module lets authorized users see federated stock across all participating organizations (opt-in basis).

What languages are supported?

The platform supports all 6 UN official languages: English, Arabic, French, Spanish, Chinese, and Russian. Arabic uses right-to-left layout. Change your language in Settings.

Is my data backed up?

The platform uses SQLite with file-based storage. For production deployments, database backups should be configured by your system administrator. The audit trail provides a complete record of all changes.

How do I report a bug or request a feature?

Contact your organization's system administrator or file an issue through the project's issue tracker.
